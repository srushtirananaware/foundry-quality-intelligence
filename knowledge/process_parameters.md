# Foundry Process Parameters

## Overview

Process parameters describe the conditions under which a manufacturing
process operates. Monitoring these parameters can help identify unusual
process behaviour and support quality analysis.

Process parameters should be interpreted together rather than treating one
parameter as the definite cause of a quality problem.

## Temperature Parameters

### Melt Temperature

Melt temperature represents the temperature of the molten material during
processing.

Changes in melt temperature can affect material flow, filling behaviour,
solidification, and the final quality of a component.

### Mold Temperature

Mold temperature describes the temperature of the mould or tooling during
the process.

It can influence heat transfer, cooling behaviour, filling characteristics,
and solidification.

## Filling Parameters

### Time to Fill

Time to fill represents the time required to fill the mould cavity.

Changes in filling time can indicate differences in material flow or process
conditions.

### Injection Pressure

Injection pressure is the pressure applied during material injection.

It can provide information about the resistance encountered during filling
and changes in process behaviour.

## Machine and Mechanical Parameters

### Closing Force

Closing force is the force used to close and maintain the mould during the
process.

Changes in closing force can affect mould stability and the conditions under
which the component is produced.

### Clamping Force

Clamping force represents the force maintaining the mould in the closed
position.

Monitoring this value can help identify changes in machine or mould
conditions.

## Plasticizing Parameters

### Plasticizing Time

Plasticizing time represents the time associated with preparing material
for the next processing cycle.

Changes in plasticizing time may indicate changes in material preparation
or machine behaviour.

### Torque

Torque measurements can provide information about the mechanical load
experienced during material preparation.

Peak torque and mean torque can be considered when analysing process
behaviour.

## Cycle Parameters

### Cycle Time

Cycle time represents the duration of a complete manufacturing cycle.

Changes in cycle time can indicate changes in machine operation, material
behaviour, or other process conditions.

## Pressure Parameters

### Back Pressure

Back pressure is a pressure applied during material preparation.

It can influence material preparation and the behaviour of the plasticizing
process.

## Position and Volume Parameters

### Screw Position

Screw position can provide information about the position of the screw at a
specific stage of the process.

Changes in screw position can be useful when analysing process consistency.

### Shot Volume

Shot volume represents the amount of material used for a processing cycle.

Changes in shot volume can indicate variations in material preparation or
process operation.

## Process Analysis

Process parameters should be analysed together with:

- Quality predictions
- Historical process behaviour
- Visual inspection results
- Machine conditions
- Material conditions
- Operator observations

A correlation between a process parameter and a quality outcome does not by
itself establish that the parameter is the direct cause of the quality
outcome.
