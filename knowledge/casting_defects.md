# Casting Defects

## Overview

Casting defects are unwanted conditions in a cast component that can affect
its appearance, dimensional accuracy, mechanical properties, or usability.

Defects can result from process conditions, material conditions, mould
conditions, solidification behaviour, gas, inclusions, or handling.

## Common Casting Defects

### Porosity

Porosity refers to cavities or voids present inside or on the surface of a
casting.

Possible contributing factors include gas entrapment, shrinkage during
solidification, inadequate feeding, or unsuitable process conditions.

### Shrinkage

Shrinkage defects can occur when metal contracts during solidification and
there is insufficient liquid metal available to compensate for the volume
reduction.

### Cold Shut

A cold shut can occur when two streams of molten metal meet but do not
properly fuse.

It can appear as a line or seam on the casting surface.

### Misrun

A misrun occurs when molten metal does not completely fill the mould cavity.

Possible contributing factors include insufficient metal temperature,
inadequate fluidity, or unsuitable filling conditions.

### Hot Tear

Hot tears are cracks that can develop during solidification when the
material is unable to accommodate contraction stresses.

### Inclusions

Inclusions are unwanted foreign materials trapped within the casting.

They may originate from oxides, slag, mould material, or other sources.

## Defect Investigation

When investigating a casting defect, inspection should consider:

- Visual appearance
- Defect location
- Defect shape
- Process parameters
- Material conditions
- Mould conditions
- Temperature conditions
- Filling and solidification behaviour

A single observed parameter should not automatically be treated as the
cause of a defect. Process data and inspection evidence should be evaluated
together.
